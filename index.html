Method and sources

The duration measure follows one Treasury: UST 4.125%, maturity November 15, 2032, CUSIP 91282CFV8, ISIN US91282CFV81. Its historical daily closing bid yields were exported from LSEG Workspace through Duke. The face value is 100, the semiannual coupon is 2.0625, and the remaining May 15 and November 15 coupon payments are discounted using each observation’s yield.

Let y be the LSEG bid yield divided by 100; t is each remaining cash flow’s time in years. Modified duration is:

D_mod = [Σ t × PV(cash flow) / Σ PV(cash flow)] / (1 + y/2)

Observation dates are used as settlement dates, with Actual/Actual coupon-period fractions and semiannual compounding. This matches the convention of Excel MDURATION(date, DATE(2032,11,15), 4.125%, yield/100, 2, 1). Duration declines partly because the bond approaches maturity. It measures sensitivity to a small change in the full yield, not just its term-premium component.

How the comparison statistics are calculated

Term premium change (bp) = 100 × (ending premium − starting premium), because the input observations are already in percent.

Dollar index change (%) = 100 × (ending index / starting index − 1).

Duration change is the ending duration minus the starting duration. Missing source observations stay missing; charts use each series’ available observations. Endpoint calculations require all three raw series to be present on the same date. No missing input is replaced by zero.
